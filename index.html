Why this matters

Anaerobic digestion plants take farm and food material and turn it into renewable gas. Ireland wants many more of them, and that is a reasonable national goal. The question this page is about is not whether these plants should exist, but where they are placed and how carefully that decision is made.

At an industrial scale, a single plant can take in hundreds of tonnes of material a day. That means heavy traffic on local roads, large volumes of digestate to store and spread on land, odour and air considerations, water and run-off risks, and gas safety. Put a plant in a well-chosen location with the right conditions, and these effects can be managed. Put the same plant in the wrong place, with gaps in the assessment, and the people living nearby carry the consequences for years.

Official committee finding and recommendation A 2026 Oireachtas committee recorded that Ireland had no AD-specific guidance or regulation for appropriate plant locations and raised concern that there were no statutory sector-specific community-engagement guidelines. It recommended clear national rules on plant locations and engagement plans. These are committee findings and recommendations, not current Irish law. [IRL-GAP-001] Read the official Oireachtas report.

That gap is why this page exists. Arup is preparing a non-statutory planning support document for the Department (DCEE), and the National Biogas Concern Group is meeting Arup to share experience and evidence. Fellow members of the group asked for international comparisons and how AD planning applications are treated elsewhere, to inform the group before that meeting. This is the group's own briefing and comparison resource, prepared by a member for the group. It gathers how other countries in Europe and beyond handle the same decision, so the group can bring evidence rather than opinion to the conversation.

A clear, predictable planning system is good for communities and for responsible developers. Communities get protection and a fair hearing. Good developers get earlier certainty, fewer disputes and a stronger case for the plants that genuinely belong where they are proposed. This is the opposite of blanket opposition.

The comparison that follows is built on official legal and regulatory sources, with each key point carrying a source link so anyone can check it. Where the evidence is incomplete, we say so rather than fill the gap. Nothing here is legal advice, and no foreign distance is presented as a proven safe number. The purpose is to inform a better national conversation about getting siting right.

One idea runs through the comparison. Community protection is a chain across site choice, complete evidence, assessment, consent, operation and enforcement, and a weakness at one stage is not cured by a rule at another.

Executive summary

What the research found about how other jurisdictions treat AD siting and applications, gathered to inform the group's meeting with Arup.

The gap

Confirmed fact Ireland has substantial general environmental controls, including route-dependent industrial emissions and best available techniques, water, nutrient and major-accident regimes. The Oireachtas committee nevertheless recorded no AD-specific national location guidance or regulation and concern about no statutory sector-specific engagement guidance. Its recommendations are not current law. So Ireland has broad general law but no verified national AD-specific method linking classification, site selection, complete evidence, participation, consent, operation and enforcement. [IRL-GAP-001; EU-IED-001; EU-BAT-001; EU-BAT-002; EU-WFD-001; EU-NIT-001; EU-SEV-001]

What the comparison shows

Confirmed comparison Jurisdictions combine different protections at different stages of the same chain, from site choice and complete evidence through assessment, consent, operation and enforcement. Examples include scoped siting rules in France and Germany, Denmark's AD-specific J205 application schedule, Ontario's whole-installation definition in its rural and agricultural framework, EU waste-treatment operating controls in scope, German monitoring requirements and Danish containment checks. The mechanisms are not directly equivalent and the audit does not rank their quality or outcomes. [FRA-SET-001; GER-SET-001; DEN-APP-001; ONT-DEF-001; EU-BAT-001; EU-BAT-002; GER-MON-001; DEN-WAT-001]

How applications are treated

Confirmed comparison Where an AD-specific route applies, several jurisdictions fix the project and its evidence early: Denmark's J205 schedule sets out required information, France's authorisation route carries siting and odour provisions, and Ontario and Germany attach defined distances and operating controls in scope. A whole-installation floor, a component or activity distance, a gas-safety distance, a formula and an effects-based test are different mechanisms, and no foreign number here is presented as a scientifically proven safe distance. [DEN-APP-001; FRA-REG-001; FRA-SET-001; GER-SET-001; ONT-DEF-001; SCI-GAP-001]

Project context

Confirmed fact Arup is preparing a non-statutory support document for planning authorities for the Department. The project scoping deck describes a main support document, assessment framework, planner checklist and best-practice examples, and asks about location, receptors, planning, environmental and community considerations, evidence, scale, feedstock and operation. The deck is a proposed scope, not completed guidance or law. [ARUP-SCOPE-001; ARUP-SCOPE-002]

What the group may raise

National Biogas Concern Group Informed by the comparison, the group has a set of evidence-grounded points to discuss with Arup, including a possible tiered approach, an application-completeness gate and whether a minimum siting floor is worth evaluating as one option among several. See the planner checklist and the points for discussion. A clearly defined siting floor would be one option to evaluate within site selection; it does not replace project-specific effects assessment and is not current Irish law or a scientifically proven safety threshold. The constructive outcome

Policy rationale A complete, predictable process can identify unsuitable sites earlier and give suitable plants assessed on complete evidence clearer conditions and decisions. The aim is to protect communities while giving responsible developers earlier certainty, not to block all development.

How to read the comparison - labels and mechanisms

Different countries protect communities in different ways. Before comparing them, it helps to know what kind of rule you are looking at, and how confident we are in each statement.

Regulatory mechanisms in plain language

  • Whole-installation floor: minimum between a legally defined installation and specified receptors.
  • Component rule: distance or control applying to a named store, tank, activity or part of a plant.
  • Gas-safety distance: external-safety separation for specified gas equipment and measurement points.
  • Formula: separation calculated using facility and receptor characteristics within a defined scope.
  • Effects test: site-specific assessment of odour, noise, air, water, habitats or risk.
  • Industrial threshold: capacity point selecting a permit or procedure, not a finding of safety or harm.
  • Best available techniques: prevention, control, monitoring and management duties within permit scope.
  • Application schedule: a standard list of evidence that must accompany an application.

France - evidence and sources
Confirmed fact - mandatory law in stated scope

Scope: ICPE 2781 authorisation regime for relevant facilities.

Headline control: Authorisation at 100 t/day in scope. Current Article 4 fixes at least 200 m from the installation, excluding raw-plant-only storage, to third-party dwellings and statutory traveller sites. Other listed receptor distances use impact and danger studies.

Operating controls: Odour source dossier and dispersion assessment, 5 ouE/m3 for no more than 175 hours/year at stated human-occupation zones, containment, firewater and eligible digestate spreading plans.

Worked example: Above 100 t/day if rubrique 2781 applies.

Limitation: The 200 m sentence does not cover every public receptor and is not a scientifically safe distance. No national sector-specific traffic threshold was verified.

Germany - evidence and sources
Confirmed fact - mandatory law and official administrative rule

Scope: Relevant non-hazardous-waste biological treatment, with separate manure categories.

Headline control: Formal permit at 50 t/day in the relevant category. TA Luft requires 300 m to existing or planned housing and 500 m for specified open facilities above 50 t/day.

Operating controls: Enclosed reception, extraction, ammonia and odour control, gas-level alarms, leak surveys, flare records, containment, expert inspection and dangerous-defect shutdown.

Worked example: Above 50 and 100 t/day numerically; category and feedstock remain necessary.

Limitation: Do not substitute the separate agricultural provision. The distances are legal floors, not safety guarantees. No federal sector-specific truck threshold was verified.

Denmark - evidence and sources
Confirmed fact - mandatory law in stated scope

Scope: J205 and industrial routes; specified livestock-farm components have separate siting rules.

Headline control: J205 above 30 t/day unless Annex 1 point 5.3(b) or 6.5 applies. Relevant recovery uses 75 t/day, or 100 t/day when anaerobic digestion is the only waste treatment.

Limitation: The 50 m provisions are not a universal standalone whole-plant setback. Full traffic, Natura, major-accident, enforcement and public-data topics need further research.

Confirmed fact - layered mandatory rules in stated scope

Scope: Activity and permit route depends on manure, plant material, waste inputs and scale.

NBCG points for discussion with Arup

The group's own suggestions to raise and discuss with Arup, informed by the comparison above. These are discussion points, not demands and not a finished policy proposal. They stay constructive and evidence-grounded, and keep the honest, non-NIMBY aim of getting siting right for communities and for responsible developers.

A possible tiered approach to raise

One idea the group may put to Arup is a proportionate set of tiers that matches scrutiny to scale and risk, rather than one blunt rule.

1. Universal baseline

Every proposal. Classification, project envelope, receptors, material flow, water, traffic, digestate, gas inventory, engagement and regulator map.

2. Enhanced risk

Triggered by effects or route. 50/75 t/day waste category, imported or centralised feedstock, sensitive receptors, vulnerable water, constrained roads, off-site digestate, expansion or cumulative development.

3. Industrial and cumulative

100 t/day design capacity or equivalent scale and risk. Full industrial schedule, alternatives, independent review, commissioning gates, public reporting and cumulative assessment.

Whether to evaluate a minimum siting floor

One option worth discussing is whether a minimum siting floor should be evaluated as part of site selection. It would be one screen among several, not the whole system.

Comparator-informed policy option

Ask the project team to evaluate a 300 m minimum from the nearest relevant potentially odorous, noisy or hazardous component to an existing or permitted dwelling on a separate property, and 500 m to schools, hospitals, nursing homes, childcare facilities, settlements or other high-sensitivity receptors. Evaluate 500 m to dwellings where routine reception, bunkers, digestate post-treatment or storage is open. [NBCG-SITE-001]

This is not current Irish law, not a scientifically proven safe distance and not sufficient without site-specific odour, noise, air, water, habitats and process-safety assessment. Greater distance must remain available where evidence requires it.

Lesson 1 - define the whole project

Observed issue
Confirmed record The two 90,000 t/year proposals had different processes, outputs, traffic, sites and local procedures. [CASE-CORR-001; CASE-LIM-001; CASE-COMP-001]
Why it matters
Inference Annual throughput alone cannot describe the planning envelope.
Relevant international comparator
Scoped comparator Denmark's J205 route requires a detailed AD-specific application schedule where it applies. [DEN-APP-001]
Proposed Irish safeguard
NBCG proposal Proportionate classification and a fixed application envelope. [NBCG-TIER-001; NBCG-APP-001]

Lesson 2 - make receptor measurements comparable

Observed issue
Confirmed record The records used boundary, source, stack and receptor measurements that were not established as equivalent, and the authorities treated the submitted odour evidence differently. [CASE-COMP-001]
Why it matters
Inference A bare distance or model headline cannot be compared without source and measurement definitions. This does not imply that distance caused either outcome or that model inputs were equivalent.
Relevant international comparator
Scoped comparators Ontario defines multiple AD components in its MDS scope; France and Germany specify odour and air controls in their stated routes. [ONT-DEF-001; FRA-ODO-001; GER-AIR-001]
Proposed Irish safeguard
NBCG proposal A certified whole-installation receptor schedule and common odour evidence protocol. [NBCG-SITE-001; NBCG-ODO-001]

Lesson 3 - resolve road feasibility at consent stage

Observed issue
Confirmed record Cork used further information and later conditions while a statutory junction-design concern remained; Limerick treated road feasibility as unresolved at consent stage. [CASE-CORR-001; CASE-LIM-001; CASE-COMP-001]
Why it matters
Inference Route geometry, land control and capacity can determine whether the proposed use can function. This does not conclude that either treatment was legally wrong.
Relevant international comparator
Scoped comparator and gaps Denmark's J205 schedule requires transport disclosure in scope. No national AD-specific traffic threshold was verified for France, Germany, the Netherlands or Ontario. [DEN-APP-001; FRA-TRA-001; GER-TRA-001; NLD-TRA-001; ONT-TRA-001]
Proposed Irish safeguard
NBCG proposal Consent-stage mass balance, route geometry, land control, capacity and enforceable movement evidence. [NBCG-TRA-001]

Lesson 4 - reconcile water, digestate and land bank

Observed issue
Confirmed record The records show different process outputs and different handling of water and drainage detail. The reviewed public land-bank material had an auditability limit. That is a limitation of this review, not an authority finding of equivalent deficiencies. [CASE-COMP-001]
Why it matters
Inference Water, digestate, traffic and land-bank effects depend on one reconciled whole-project balance.
Relevant international comparator
Scoped comparators France, Germany and Denmark provide route-specific containment and water controls; France, Denmark and Northern Ireland provide digestate or nutrient controls in scope. [FRA-WAT-002; GER-WAT-001; GER-WAT-002; DEN-WAT-001; FRA-DIG-001; DEN-DIG-001; NIR-WAT-001]
Proposed Irish safeguard
NBCG proposal Reconciled balances, land-bank evidence, drainage, containment and accident response. [NBCG-DIG-001; NBCG-SAF-001]

Lesson 5 - publish a reproducible decision record

Observed issue
Confirmed record Cork issued 33 further-information items and 57 conditions; Limerick refused on five grounds without formal further information. [CASE-CORR-001; CASE-LIM-001]
Why it matters
Inference Readers need a reproducible record of what was resolved, refused or deferred. The contrast does not prove causation, legal error or that further information or conditions would have cured refusal grounds. [CASE-COMP-002]
Relevant international comparator
Scoped comparators Denmark's schedule, odour commissioning and containment checks, and German monitoring controls illustrate specified evidence and verification in their stated routes. [DEN-APP-001; DEN-ODO-001; DEN-WAT-001; GER-MON-001]
Proposed Irish safeguard
NBCG proposal A completeness gate and one controlled decision record. [NBCG-APP-001; NBCG-COORD-001]
