Why this matters

Anaerobic digestion plants take farm and food material and turn it into renewable gas. Ireland wants many more of them, and that is a reasonable national goal. The question this page is about is not whether these plants should exist, but where they are placed and how carefully that decision is made.

At an industrial scale, a single plant can take in hundreds of tonnes of material a day. That means heavy traffic on local roads, large volumes of digestate to store and spread on land, odour and air considerations, water and run-off risks, and gas safety. Put a plant in a well-chosen location with the right conditions, and these effects can be managed. Put the same plant in the wrong place, with gaps in the assessment, and the people living nearby carry the consequences for years.

Right now Ireland has no dedicated national method for deciding where anaerobic digestion plants should and should not go. There is no common starting point for how far a plant should sit from homes, schools and other sensitive places, no standard list of evidence every application must contain, and no sector-specific requirement for early engagement with the community. A 2026 Oireachtas committee confirmed this gap and recommended that it be closed.

That gap is why this page exists. The Department has asked Arup to prepare support guidance for planning authorities, and the National Biogas Concern Group has been invited to contribute. This page gathers how other countries in Europe and beyond handle the same decision, so the group can bring evidence rather than opinion to that conversation.

A clear, predictable planning system is good for communities and for responsible developers. Communities get protection and a fair hearing. Good developers get earlier certainty, fewer disputes and a stronger case for the plants that genuinely belong where they are proposed. This is the opposite of blanket opposition.

The comparison that follows is built on official legal and regulatory sources, with each key point carrying a source link so anyone can check it. Where the evidence is incomplete, we say so rather than fill the gap. Nothing here is legal advice, and no foreign distance is presented as a proven safe number. The purpose is to inform a better national conversation about getting siting right.

Executive summary

The one-page version, for anyone short of time.

The situation

Ireland is encouraging a large expansion of anaerobic digestion under the National Biomethane Strategy, but has no dedicated national method for deciding where these plants go. There is no common siting starting point, no standard application-evidence schedule and no sector-specific community-engagement requirement. A 2026 Oireachtas committee confirmed this gap and recommended it be closed. The Department has commissioned Arup to prepare non-statutory support guidance for planning authorities, and the National Biogas Concern Group has been invited to contribute.

What the international comparison shows

  1. Other jurisdictions protect communities through a mix of mechanisms, not one number: France sets a 200 m dwelling floor, Germany 300 m rising to 500 m for open facilities, Ontario 200 m and 450 m by receptor type, while Denmark and the Netherlands rely more on component distances, detailed application schedules and effects tests.
  2. A distance rule is never used on its own. It sits alongside odour modelling, containment, monitoring and operating controls. No jurisdiction treats a setback as a guarantee of safety.
  3. The whole installation matters, not just the digester tank. Ontario's rules expressly include feedstock and digestate stores, loading, flares and separators.
  4. Several jurisdictions require a standard evidence pack up front. Denmark's application schedule is a clear worked model.
  5. Ireland's gap is specifically in planning method, not environmental law. Strong EU-derived controls exist, but they are not organised into a consistent, sector-specific siting framework.

What the group proposes

  • A proportionate, tiered national framework scaling scrutiny to size and risk, with no bypass through feedstock labels.
  • A standard application-evidence schedule at industrial scale: project envelope, receptor map, mass and nutrient balances, traffic, water, gas inventory and monitoring.
  • Clear receptor definitions and measurement from the nearest relevant component, with a national minimum siting floor offered as a policy option that is always supplemented by site-specific assessment, never a substitute for it.
  • Enforceable odour, digestate, water, traffic and gas-safety controls, independent verification, public monitoring and incident disclosure.
  • Early, structured community engagement and coordination across the planning, environmental, roads, water, agriculture and safety regulators.

The bottom line

A clear, predictable siting system protects communities and gives responsible developers earlier certainty. The group's aim is a robust framework that gets the right plants into the right places, not a block on all development.

Opening thesis and Irish context

In short: Ireland already has strong environmental law, but no single national method for siting these plants well. This is the gap the group wants to help close.

Ireland has important planning and environmental controls. The identified gap is a common national anaerobic-digestion method for classification, early site screening, consent-stage evidence, regulator coordination, engagement and post-consent transparency.

Different countries protect communities in different ways. Before comparing them, it helps to know what kind of rule you are looking at, and how confident we are in each statement.

Regulatory mechanisms in plain language

  • Whole-installation floor: minimum between a legally defined installation and specified receptors.
  • Component rule: distance or control applying to a named store, tank, activity or part of a plant.
  • Gas-safety distance: external-safety separation for specified gas equipment and measurement points.
  • Formula: separation calculated using facility and receptor characteristics within a defined scope.
  • Effects test: site-specific assessment of odour, noise, air, water, habitats or risk.
  • Industrial threshold: capacity point selecting a permit or procedure, not a finding of safety or harm.
  • Best available techniques: prevention, control, monitoring and management duties within permit scope.
  • Application schedule: a standard list of evidence that must accompany an application.

France

Confirmed fact - mandatory law in stated scope

Scope: ICPE 2781 authorisation regime for relevant facilities.

Headline control: Authorisation at 100 t/day in scope. Current Article 4 fixes at least 200 m from the installation, excluding raw-plant-only storage, to third-party dwellings and statutory traveller sites. Other listed receptor distances use impact and danger studies.

Operating controls: Odour source dossier and dispersion assessment, 5 ouE/m3 for no more than 175 hours/year at stated human-occupation zones, containment, firewater and eligible digestate spreading plans.

Worked example: Above 100 t/day if rubrique 2781 applies.

Limitation: The 200 m sentence does not cover every public receptor and is not a scientifically safe distance. No national sector-specific traffic threshold was verified.

Germany

Confirmed fact - mandatory law and official administrative rule

Scope: Relevant non-hazardous-waste biological treatment, with separate manure categories.

Headline control: Formal permit at 50 t/day in the relevant category. TA Luft requires 300 m to existing or planned housing and 500 m for specified open facilities above 50 t/day.

Operating controls: Enclosed reception, extraction, ammonia and odour control, gas-level alarms, leak surveys, flare records, containment, expert inspection and dangerous-defect shutdown.

Worked example: Above 50 and 100 t/day numerically; category and feedstock remain necessary.

Limitation: Do not substitute the separate agricultural provision. The distances are legal floors, not safety guarantees. No federal sector-specific truck threshold was verified.

Denmark

Confirmed fact - mandatory law in stated scope

Scope: J205 and industrial routes; specified livestock-farm components have separate siting rules.

Headline control: J205 above 30 t/day unless Annex 1 point 5.3(b) or 6.5 applies. Relevant recovery uses 75 t/day, or 100 t/day when anaerobic digestion is the only waste treatment.

Worked example: Above listed numbers, but waste, recovery, animal-waste and farm or standalone status decide the route.

Limitation: The 50 m provisions are not a universal standalone whole-plant setback. Full traffic, Natura, major-accident, enforcement and public-data topics need further research.

Netherlands

Confirmed fact - layered mandatory rules in stated scope

Scope: Activity and permit route depends on manure, plant material, waste inputs and scale.

Headline control: Qualifying non-hazardous-waste recovery above 100 t/day can enter the IPPC route. Bkl tables retain 100 m and 50 m distances for specified activities and sizes. A separate gas bag or liquefied-biogas tank has a 50 m site-boundary safety distance.

Limitation: Activity tables, gas safety and effects tests are not equivalent. Several national digestate, water, process-emission, accident and enforcement topics need further research.

Headline control: Publication 853 uses 200 m to dwellings and Type A uses, 450 m to Type B uses, 20 m to rear or side lot lines and 40 m to roads. The ordinary reduction guideline does not reduce the anaerobic-digestion distances.

Worked example: Tonnage alone does not select the Minimum Distance Separation, farm or Environmental Compliance Approval route.

Water, karst, drainage and containment

Confirmed facts

Proposed Irish tiered framework

Drawing those lessons together, here is a practical way Ireland could close the gap: proportionate tiers that match scrutiny to scale and risk, rather than one blunt rule.

1. Universal baseline

Every proposal. Classification, project envelope, receptors, material flow, water, traffic, digestate, gas inventory, engagement and regulator map.

2. Enhanced risk

Triggered by effects or route. 50/75 t/day waste category, imported or centralised feedstock, sensitive receptors, vulnerable water, constrained roads, off-site digestate, expansion or cumulative development.

3. Industrial and cumulative

100 t/day design capacity or equivalent scale and risk. Full industrial schedule, alternatives, independent review, commissioning gates, public reporting and cumulative assessment.

National Biogas Concern Group recommendation

Apply all three levels without allowing a crop, manure or by-product label to bypass planning scrutiny. Separate law still decides waste, industrial-emissions and animal-by-product routes. [NBCG-TIER-001]

Industrial-tier application schedule

  1. Annual and maximum daily design intake, operating days, feedstocks, legal-status assumptions and expansion.
  2. Fixed process envelope and mapped active components.
  3. Site-selection report with reasonable alternatives and constraints.
  4. Certified boundary, component and receptor schedule with measurement points.
  5. Mass, water, carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus balances.
  6. Odour, air and noise inventories, models, guarantees and abnormal cases.
  7. Drainage, containment, groundwater, karst, flood and firewater concept.
  8. Digestate storage, quality, parcel-level land bank, existing loads and contingency.
  9. Annual, average-day, peak-day and peak-hour traffic with payloads and every route.
  10. Hazardous inventory, credible accidents, emergency access and operator competence.
  11. Habitats source-pathway-receptor and in-combination schedule.
  12. Commissioning, monitoring, public reporting, complaints, incidents and escalation.

National Biogas Concern Group recommendation [NBCG-APP-001]

Minimum siting floor as an option to test

Comparator-informed policy option

Ask the project team to evaluate a 300 m minimum from the nearest relevant potentially odorous, noisy or hazardous component to an existing or permitted dwelling on a separate property, and 500 m to schools, hospitals, nursing homes, childcare facilities, settlements or other high-sensitivity receptors. Evaluate 500 m to dwellings where routine reception, bunkers, digestate post-treatment or storage is open. [NBCG-SITE-001]

This is not current Irish law, not a scientifically proven safe distance and not sufficient without site-specific odour, noise, air, water, habitats and process-safety assessment. Greater distance must remain available where evidence requires it.

Ten precise recommendations

If the framework is the shape of the solution, these are the specific, checkable asks the group puts forward.

1. Proportionate tiers

Universal baseline, enhanced risk tier and industrial or cumulative tier. No feedstock-label bypass. [NBCG-TIER-001]

2. Application schedule

Fixed project envelope, receptors, balances, traffic, land bank, effects, containment, safety, alternatives and monitoring. [NBCG-APP-001]

3. Defined siting option

Evaluate 300 m and 500 m floors with component and receptor definitions, plus assessment and power to require more. [NBCG-SITE-001]

4. Odour, air and noise

Complete inventory, prevention, enclosure, local modelling, commissioning, routine checks, complaints and correction. [NBCG-ODO-001]

5. Traffic reconciliation

Mass-balanced annual and peak traffic, all routes, cumulative projects and enforceable limits. [NBCG-TRA-001]

6. Digestate and water

Covered storage, lawful parcel-level land bank, nitrogen and phosphorus balance, catchment effects, records and audit. [NBCG-DIG-001]

7. Process safety

Gas inventory, fire, explosion, hydrogen sulphide, containment, firewater, emergency response and competence. [NBCG-SAF-001]

8. Monitoring and enforcement

Commissioning, independent verification, public non-confidential data, incidents, complaints, raw records and escalation. [NBCG-MON-001]

9. Early engagement

Engage before design is fixed, disclose alternatives and publish a response and design-change record. [NBCG-ENG-001]

10. One controlled record

Shared dataset and responsibility map across relevant authorities, with each legal role preserved. [NBCG-COORD-001]

Inference

The records show different evidence handling, measurement bases and use of conditions. They support a common application schedule and decision record. [CASE-COMP-001]

Caveats and evidence limits

  • Official law and guidance can change after the evidence review date.
  • Foreign legal terms and translations require original-language checking before provision-level reliance.
  • Waste and Industrial Emissions Directive routes depend on feedstock, waste or by-product status, treatment purpose and route, and co-located operations, not tonnage alone.
  • Denmark's full traffic, Natura, major-accident, enforcement and public-data review is incomplete. Further research required. [DEN-GAP-001]
  • Several Dutch national digestate, water, process-emission, accident, monitoring and enforcement topics remain incomplete. Further research required. [NLD-GAP-001]
  • The New Zealand official whole-Act portal had an extraction limitation during the audit.
  • No transferable peer-reviewed universal setback, traffic threshold or noise threshold was identified. [SCI-GAP-001]
  • Scientific studies support source inventories and verification, not universal emission factors or distances.
  • The Irish cases were live appeals at the stated check date and are not final precedent.
  • This page provides policy information, not legal advice or a case-specific planning assessment.
